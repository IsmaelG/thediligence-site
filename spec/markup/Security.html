Keys and models

A pasted key is the last resort. No key near an agent.

Model-written code is the riskiest thing in the system, so it is the one place that never holds a secret. You connect your provider through a third party, a run gets a token that is worth nothing outside this run, and a broker does the rest.

  1. 1 · YouYour provider account

    You approve access on your provider's own screen where the provider allows it. Where it does not, you can paste a key as a last resort: it is encrypted, never visible to agents, and you can delete it in one click.

    Your key, when you must paste one, is encrypted
  2. 2 · RunWorkflow

    Starts the run and mints a short-lived token, valid for this workspace, this target, this budget and these models.

    Holds a token that expires with the run
  3. 3 · WorkSandbox

    Where the agents read and write. Documents are mounted read-only. Only a short list of hosts can be reached.

    Holds the token. No credential, ever
  4. 4 · SwapConnection broker

    Turns the token into one call to your provider through your connection, checks the budget, and records the cost.

    Reaches your provider one call at a time
  5. 5 · ModelYour provider

    Sees an ordinary call from your account. The bill lands with you, not with us.

    Your account, your terms

Data classes

Every document has a class. The class decides who may read it.

A class is set when a document comes in and shown on every row of the ledger. Before you launch, the plan tells you which provider will read which class.

ClassWhat it isWhich models may read itWhere it goes
PublicWebsites, filings, press, anything anyone can open.Any model you connect.Your ledger, with the source id and the date retrieved.
Target-confidentialWhat the target shared with you: decks, data-room exports, financials, call notes.Only providers that do not retain or train on your traffic, under a data processing agreement.Your workspace only. Deleted with the target. Exports are watermarked.
Fund-internalYour thesis, partner notes, term sheets, references.Same as above, and only for the targets and runs you allow.Your vault, hosted by us and encrypted in your browser, or kept in Obsidian from Pro. A note reaches a run only when you share it.

Logs, traces and analytics carry ids and costs. They never carry evidence, prompts or keys.

Controls

What we have built, what is on its way.

Each control carries a status. At launch means it ships with the first release. Next and Later are on our roadmap, and we do not describe them as done.

At launch

Access

Sign-in by one-time email code, rate-limited and usable once. Roles per workspace. The database itself refuses to show one workspace the rows of another.

At launch

Provider connections

You approve access on your provider's own screen where the provider allows it, and you can revoke it at any time. Where it does not, a pasted key is the last resort: encrypted, never visible to agents, deleted in one click.

At launch

Sandboxes

One isolated sandbox per branch of a run. Evidence is mounted read-only. Each step reaches only the hosts it needs: search, the target's own domains, package registries.

At launch

Untrusted documents

A founder deck can contain instructions. We treat every document as data, never as a command, and agents have no tool that could act on one.

At launch

Run log and retention

Every tool call, decision and cost goes to an append-only log you can replay. Evidence goes when the target does, and a retention policy can be set per workspace.

At launch

Sharing and export

Links are scoped to the workspace and expire. File links are signed and short-lived. Exports carry the workspace and the date. Everything can be exported.

Next

Europe mode

Sandboxes hosted in the EU first. A Europe-only mode follows, where models are restricted to European regions or to a European provider.

At launch

Vault

Hosted by us and encrypted in your browser: we store ciphertext and cannot read it. From Pro, an Obsidian plugin keeps notes in your own vault instead, and we hold no key to them. Agents cannot read either, unless a member shares a note for one target, and the share is logged.

Later

Single sign-on and audit log

SAML sign-on for the whole team, and an audit log of who opened what, for the Fund plan.

Limits on the agents

What an agent can never do.

These are rules built into the product, not promises in a policy.

  • Hold or read your provider credentials.
  • Send, post or email anything. Agents draft, people send.
  • Reach a host that is not on the list for that step.
  • Write a claim without a source, unless it is graded C and says so.
  • Spend past the budget you set.
  • Give a verdict without a person approving it at the gate.
